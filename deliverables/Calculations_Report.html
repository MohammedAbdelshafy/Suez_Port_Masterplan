CALC-01
Design Vessel Parameters
Objective Establish the governing design vessel dimensions
The design vessel (or "vessel of reference") is the largest vessel expected to call at the port on a regular basis. All navigation geometry, berthing dimensions, and structural tolerances are derived from its principal dimensions. PIANC terminology uses: Lmax (maximum length), bmax (maximum beam), dmax (maximum draft).
PIANC Reference: PIANC Harbour Approach Channels Design Guidelines (2014) — Section 3.2, Design Vessel Selection.
SymbolValueDescriptionUnit
Lmax250Maximum length overallm
bmax32Maximum beam (moulded breadth)m
dmax12Maximum operating draftm
Design Vessel Class
Panamax / Cape-Size Tankerequivalent
Governs all channel, basin, and berth geometry. All dimensions in this report are referenced to Lmax, bmax, and dmax.
CALC-02
Navigation Channel Depth — UKC Method
Objective Determine minimum dredged depth for safe navigation
The Under-Keel Clearance (UKC) method ensures that the minimum vertical distance between the deepest point of the vessel's keel and the bed of the navigation channel is maintained at all times, accounting for vessel squat, waves, and bed tolerance.
Governing Equation — UKC Method (PIANC 2014)
h = dmax × (1 + UKC%) + Δsquat + Δwave + Δsounding
SymbolValueDescriptionUnit
dmax12.0Maximum vessel draftm
UKC%15%Net Under-Keel Clearance (PIANC min. 10–15%)—
Δsquat0.5Squat and dynamic response allowancem
Δwave0.0Wave allowance (sheltered channel)m
Δsounding0.1Survey and sounding uncertaintym
Step 1
UKC allowance = dmax × 0.15 = 12.0 × 0.15 = 1.80 m
Step 2
Draft with UKC = 12.0 + 1.80 = 13.80 m
Step 3
Total required depth = 13.80 + 0.50 + 0.10 = 14.40 m
Step 4
Adopted dredged depth = −13.4 m CD (nominal programme value; refined in detailed design)
Adopted Dredged Depth
−13.4m CD
This depth accommodates the design vessel at maximum draft with PIANC-compliant UKC and dynamic allowances. Negative value refers to Chart Datum (CD).
CALC-03
Navigation Channel Width — PIANC Method
Objective Calculate the minimum channel width for one-way traffic
Channel width is governed by the design vessel beam multiplied by a factor that accounts for bank effects, currents, restricted visibility, and vessel manoeuvring envelope. PIANC recommends a minimum one-way width factor of 6–8 × bmax for confined channels with tug assistance.
Governing Equation — PIANC (2014) Concept One-Way
W = n × bmax
SymbolValueDescriptionUnit
n7.0PIANC width factor (one-way, moderate conditions)—
bmax32Maximum vessel beamm
Step 1
W = 7.0 × 32 = 224 m
Step 2
Comparison: PIANC minimum = 6.0 × 32 = 192 m → Adopted 224 m provides 16.7% margin
Channel Width (Adopted)
224m
PASS — 224 m ≥ PIANC minimum 192 m (6.0 × bmax). Adopted width factor 7.0 provides conservative margin for tug-assisted navigation in moderate crosswind conditions.
CALC-04
Turning Basin Diameter — PIANC Method
Objective Determine the minimum turning basin diameter for tug-assisted manoeuvring
The turning basin allows vessels to reverse direction after berthing or before departure. PIANC recommends a minimum circular basin diameter of 1.5 × Lmax when tug assistance is available, or up to 2.0 × Lmax for unassisted turning.
Governing Equation — PIANC (2014) Tug-Assisted
Dbasin = 1.5 × Lmax

CALC-05
Breakwater Armour Mass — Hudson Formula
Objective Size the breakwater armour unit (median armour mass W50)
The Hudson formula (Shore Protection Manual, USACE) is the standard empirical method for sizing rough rubble-mound armour units. It relates the required armour mass to the design wave height, armour unit shape/placement (KD), specific gravity, and slope.
Hudson Formula (SPM, USACE 1984)
W₅₀ = ρs · Hs³ / [KD · (Sr − 1)³ · cot(α)]
SymbolValueDescriptionUnit
ρs2,650Armour rock densitykg/m³
ρw1,025Seawater densitykg/m³
Hs4.5Significant design wave height (50-year return)m
KD4.0Stability coefficient — rough quarry stone, randomly placed, trunk section, breaking waves—
Sr2.585Specific gravity of armour = ρs/ρw = 2650/1025—
cot(α)2.0Seaward slope (1V:2H → cot = 2.0)—
Step 1
Sr = 2650 / 1025 = 2.585
Step 2
(Sr − 1) = 2.585 − 1 = 1.585
Step 3
(Sr − 1)³ = 1.585³ = 3.982
Step 4
Hs³ = 4.5³ = 91.125 m³
Step 5
Denominator = KD × (Sr−1)³ × cot(α) = 4.0 × 3.982 × 2.0 = 31.856
Step 6
W₅₀ = ρs × Hs³ / Denominator = 2650 × 91.125 / 31.856
Step 7
W₅₀ = 241,481 / 31.856 = 7,580 kg ≈ 7.6 t
Median Armour Mass W₅₀
7.6tonnes
Individual armour stones with median mass of 7.6 t are required. Stones in the range W₅₀ × [0.5, 2.0] are acceptable for primary armour layer per SPM guidance.
CALC-06
Breakwater Crest Elevation
Objective Set the breakwater crest level to limit overtopping
Crest elevation is set to limit wave overtopping rates to acceptable levels per EurOtop (2018). For port breakwaters the target is typically q ≤ 0.1 l/s/m. The simplified rule-of-thumb crest level of approximately 1.5–2.0 × Hs above MHW is used at concept stage.
Concept Estimation (EurOtop 2018 — simplified)
Rc ≈ 1.5 × Hs (concept stage)
Step 1
Rc = 1.5 × Hs = 1.5 × 4.5 = 6.75 m above MHW
Step 2
MHW at Suez ≈ +0.2 m CD (tidal range ~0.4 m)
Step 3
Crest level = 6.75 + 0.20 ≈ 6.95 m CD → Adopted: +7.0 m CD
Breakwater Crest Level (Adopted)
+7.0m CD
PASS — Crest at +7.0 m CD provides a freeboard of approximately 6.8 m above MHW. To be verified with detailed EurOtop overtopping calculations at tender stage.
