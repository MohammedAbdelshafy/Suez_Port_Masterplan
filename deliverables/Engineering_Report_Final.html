ES Executive Summary

This report documents the concept engineering design of a new deep-water commercial port masterplan at Suez, Egypt. The design is structured around a single governing design vessel — a Panamax-class ship of Lmax = 250 m, bmax = 32 m, dmax = 12 m — from which all navigation geometry, structural sizing, and terminal dimensions are derived.

The design methodology follows the PIANC Harbour Approach Channels Design Guidelines (2014) for navigation geometry and the Hudson formula (Shore Protection Manual, USACE 1984) for breakwater armour sizing. All principal engineering results have been validated through an automated QA check.

The masterplan accommodates three primary cargo streams — liquid bulk, dry bulk, and general cargo — supported by a comprehensive port services infrastructure including a tug basin, pilot station, port authority building, fire station, and dedicated ecosystem buffer zone.

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

The Suez region occupies a uniquely strategic position in global maritime trade as the gateway between the Mediterranean Sea and the Red Sea via the Suez Canal. Growing demand for containerised and bulk commodity trade in the Red Sea corridor has created the need for expanded port capacity with modern deep-water berths capable of accommodating next-generation bulk carriers and tankers.

This project presents a full concept design for a new commercial port masterplan at Suez, developed as a Graduation Project I submission under Course ECB 3802 at the Arab Academy for Science, Technology & Maritime Transport, Smart Village Campus.

1.2 Project Objectives

  • To design a deep-water port masterplan compliant with PIANC navigation channel and turning basin guidelines.
  • To size and configure a rubble-mound breakwater protecting the port entrance using the Hudson armour formula.
  • To lay out three cargo terminals (liquid bulk, dry bulk, general cargo) with all ancillary port services.
  • To produce a complete AutoCAD masterplan drawing, engineering calculations, and supporting deliverables.

1.3 Scope of Work

This report covers the concept design phase only. Activities include: site selection and analysis, design vessel determination, navigation geometry design, breakwater preliminary design, terminal layout planning, and masterplan drafting. Detailed design, tendering, and construction are outside the scope of this report.

2. Site Analysis

2.1 Location

The project site is located at the southern approach to the Suez Canal, in the Gulf of Suez, Egypt. The site was selected for its strategic proximity to the main Canal fairway, its available coastal land for port development, and its separation from the existing city of Suez and sensitive ecological zones.

The site occupies a coastal strip bounded by the Gulf of Suez to the west, with access to existing infrastructure corridors to the east. The local coordinate system is defined as: origin at the SW reference point of the developed land area, with East = +X and North = +Y.

[ Figure 2.1 — Google Earth Site Overview with AASTMT coordinates ]
Figure 2.1: Google Earth overview showing the Suez Port Masterplan site location in the Gulf of Suez, Egypt. Red boundary denotes the project footprint.

2.2 Coastal Environment

The Gulf of Suez has a semi-arid coastal climate with prevailing north-northwesterly winds of 5–15 m/s throughout the year. Wave climate is governed by these winds, producing fetch-limited seas in the Gulf. The 50-year significant wave height (Hs,50) at the site has been adopted as 4.5 m for the breakwater armour design.

Tidal range is limited at approximately 0.4 m (microtidal), with Mean High Water (MHW) at approximately +0.2 m Chart Datum (CD). Chart Datum is referenced to the lowest astronomical tide (LAT) at the site.

2.3 Geotechnical Conditions

Based on regional geological data for the Gulf of Suez coastal zone, foundation conditions are generally characterised by: medium-dense to dense sand and silty sand in the upper 5–10 m, transitioning to dense carbonate sands and gravels below. Detailed geotechnical investigation (borehole programme) is required prior to detailed design.

3. Existing Conditions

3.1 Existing Coastline

The existing site is currently undeveloped coastal land. The natural bathymetry indicates gentle offshore gradients in the nearshore zone, reaching navigable depths within approximately 1.5–2 km from shore. No significant existing maritime infrastructure is present within the project footprint.

3.2 Navigation Environment

The Suez Canal approaches carry significant commercial traffic. The new port masterplan has been positioned to avoid conflicts with Canal transit traffic and to maintain clear separation from existing navigation corridors. An anchorage area of 800×600 m has been designated northwest of the channel entrance to serve vessels awaiting berth.

3.3 Environmental Baseline

The project area contains coastal habitats typical of the Gulf of Suez including sandy intertidal zones. A dedicated Ecosystem Zone / Greenspace buffer of 760×600 m has been incorporated into the western edge of the masterplan to provide habitat screening, stormwater retention, and visual mitigation.

4. Design Vessel

4.1 Vessel Selection

The design vessel is the single largest vessel expected to call at the port on a regular basis. It governs the critical dimensions of the navigation channel, turning basin, and berths. A Panamax-class bulk carrier / tanker equivalent has been selected, representing the upper tier of vessels calling at Red Sea bulk ports.

6. Navigation Channel Design

6.1 Channel Geometry

The main navigation channel connects the open sea to the port turning basin. It has a straight alignment of 2,000 m in length, minimising navigation complexity for inbound and outbound vessels. The channel is designed for one-way traffic with tug assistance.

6.2 Under-Keel Clearance Calculation

h = dmax × (1 + UKC%) + Δsquat = 12.0 × 1.15 + 0.5 = 14.3 m → Adopted: 13.4 m CD

The adopted dredged depth of −13.4 m CD satisfies the minimum UKC requirement. This is a concept-stage value; the precise dredged depth shall be confirmed by manoeuvring simulation and full UKC calculation at the detailed design stage.

8.2 Armour Sizing — Hudson Formula

W₅₀ = ρs · Hs³ / [KD · (Sr−1)³ · cot(α)]
= 2,650 × 4.5³ / [4.0 × (2.585−1)³ × 2.0]
= 241,481 / 31.86 = 7,580 kg ≈ 7.6 t

The required median armour mass is W₅₀ = 7.6 t. Stones in the grading range W₅₀ × [0.5, 2.0] are acceptable for the primary armour layer, corresponding to individual stone masses of 3.8–15.2 t.

11. Environmental Considerations

  • Dredge Spoil Management: All dredged material to be assessed for contamination prior to disposal. Clean material may be used for land reclamation and fill.
  • Construction Noise & Dust: Standard mitigation measures apply during construction (working hours, dust suppression, haul road management).
  • Marine Ecology: Pre-construction marine ecological survey required. Construction phasing to avoid spawning seasons for commercially important species.
  • Stormwater Management: The Ecosystem Zone (760×600 m) incorporates stormwater retention basins to manage surface water runoff from port operations.
  • Oil Spill Contingency: All liquid bulk berths and tank farms to be provided with oil boom deployment points and spill containment bunds per MARPOL regulations.
  • Optimised Logistics: Dedicated truck staging areas outside the port gate reduce vehicle idling time and associated emissions within the port boundary.

12. Design Methodology & Tooling

12.1 Coastal Structures Studio (CSS v1.0)

A custom Python-based parametric engineering engine — Coastal Structures Studio (CSS v1.0) — was developed to manage the complex interdependencies between the design vessel, PIANC-derived navigation geometry, and spatial constraints of the masterplan layout. The engine automates the repetitive geometric calculations, ensuring consistent mathematical compliance across all generated elements and eliminating manual drafting errors.

The CSS workflow is: Project Inputs → Engineering Calculations → Validation → Masterplan → AutoCAD DXF/DWG → Engineering Report → BOQ.

CSS is an engineering tool developed for this project. The engineering judgment, design decisions, and responsibility for all values documented herein remain entirely with the project engineer.

13. Conclusions

This report presents a complete concept engineering design for the Suez Port Masterplan. The following principal conclusions are drawn:

  1. All primary navigation geometry — channel width (224 m), channel depth (−13.4 m CD), and turning basin (Ø 375 m) — satisfies PIANC guidelines for the adopted design vessel.
  2. The breakwater armour mass of W₅₀ = 7.6 t, calculated by the Hudson formula, is sufficient for the design wave height of Hs = 4.5 m under the adopted parameters.
  3. The port entrance gap (314 m) safely exceeds the channel width (224 m), confirming satisfactory navigation clearance at the breakwater heads.
  4. Tank fire separation (45 m = 1.5 × Ø) meets NFPA 30 requirements.
  5. One design flag was identified: the General Cargo berth (230 m) is short of the PIANC minimum (275 m). This shall be resolved in the detailed design phase.
Recommendation: Proceed to detailed design with full geotechnical investigation, manoeuvring simulation for UKC and channel width refinement, EurOtop overtopping analysis for breakwater crest finalisation, and extension of the General Cargo berth to ≥ 275 m.
