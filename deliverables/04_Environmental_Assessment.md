# Suez Port Masterplan — Environmental Assessment (Concept Level)

**Coastal Structures Studio (CSS v1.0)** · ECB 3802 · Mohamed Abdelshafy
(20107979) · Supervisor: Dr. Walid Al-Amri.

> Concept-level environmental scoping to accompany the masterplan. Values are
> consistent with the single-source calculation engine. A full EIA with field
> baseline data is required before any construction decision.

## 1. Scope
This assessment scopes the principal environmental aspects of constructing and
operating the concept Suez port and identifies mitigation and monitoring
measures. It is qualitative/semi-quantitative at concept stage.

## 2. Dredging & sediment management
- **Capital dredging** of the 2 000 m × 224 m channel (−13.4 m CD) and the
  375 m turning basin is the dominant earthworks item — on the order of
  **7.5 × 10⁶ m³**. Maintenance dredging will recur as the channel infills.
- *Aspects:* turbidity plumes, benthic habitat removal, release of any
  contaminated sediment, disposal of dredged material.
- *Mitigation:* silt curtains around active dredging; cutter/TSHD selection to
  limit overspill; beneficial reuse of clean material for reclamation and
  bunds; designated, licensed offshore disposal; dredge only within the
  surveyed prism.

## 3. Water quality
- *Aspects:* elevated suspended solids during dredging and reclamation; reduced
  flushing within the breakwater-enclosed basin; operational runoff from
  terminals.
- *Mitigation:* turbidity monitoring with trigger levels; maintain entrance
  geometry for adequate basin exchange; first-flush stormwater treatment;
  oil/water separators at fuelling and the oil terminal.

## 4. Sediment transport & coastal morphology
- *Aspects:* the twin breakwaters and dredged channel interrupt littoral drift
  — accretion updrift, erosion downdrift, and channel infilling. The natural
  **sand spit** on the seaward flank indicates an active longshore system.
- *Mitigation:* littoral-drift and morphological modelling; sand-bypassing
  provision; monitor adjacent shorelines; design maintenance-dredging budget.

## 5. Marine ecology
- *Aspects:* loss/disturbance of benthic habitat in the dredge footprint;
  underwater noise from piling and dredging; risk to fish and any
  seagrass/coral in the Gulf/Red Sea ecotone; invasive species via ballast.
- *Mitigation:* habitat survey and sensitive-receptor mapping; soft-start
  piling and seasonal restrictions; ballast-water management compliance
  (BWM Convention); habitat compensation where required.

## 6. Air quality & noise
- *Aspects:* construction plant and dredger emissions; operational emissions
  from ships at berth, cargo handling (especially **dry-bulk silos** — dust)
  and road traffic; community noise near gates and roads.
- *Mitigation:* dust suppression and enclosed conveying at the silo field;
  shore-power provision where feasible; low-emission plant; noise bunds /
  buffer zones between operations and any sensitive boundary.

## 7. Spill prevention & emergency response
- *Aspects:* hydrocarbon spills at the **oil terminal** and **liquid-bulk tank
  farm**; firewater runoff.
- *Mitigation:* fully **bunded tank farm** (containment ≥ largest tank volume),
  pipe-corridor segregation and safety buffer (in the layout); spill booms and
  a tier-1 response plan; fire-fighting and contained firewater drainage.

## 8. Sustainability recommendations
- Beneficial reuse of dredged material; shore power / electrification;
  renewable generation on the expansion parcel; rainwater harvesting; green
  buffer planting; designed-in monitoring (water quality, shoreline, noise,
  air) from day one.

## 9. Environmental buffer zones
Buffer zones and a utility corridor are reserved in the masterplan to separate
hazardous (oil, tank farm) and dust-generating (silos) operations from the
boundary and from passenger/general areas, and to host monitoring and
landscaping.

## 10. Conclusion
No fatal environmental flaw is identified at concept level. The dominant issues
— dredging/turbidity, littoral-drift interruption and hydrocarbon-spill risk —
are manageable with standard mitigation. A full EIA with baseline met-ocean,
water-quality, sediment and ecological surveys is required before detailed
design.
